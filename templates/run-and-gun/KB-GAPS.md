# KB gaps found building FIREBASE

Each line: what was asked, what the KB said, what was true, the evidence.
Tool answers are from c64-kb 0.29.0 (KB data 843) on the branch graph
`c64_rg`, 2026-09-26. Measured figures are VICE x64sc 3.10 (PAL C64C and
`-model ntsc`), from this starter's `make shot check` unless named.

1. **plan-budget, row_map_redraw.** Asked: `plan-budget` on the fingerprint. Said: row_map_redraw 13,304 cycles summed into every play frame (range 36,916-47,124, over the frame). True: the redraw runs on one frame in eight, the one YSCROLL wraps on, and the game runs no logic then. Measured: frames that run the logic take at most 3,049 cycles (PAL) and 3,071 (NTSC) with the IRQs; the redraw is 14,100 (PAL) and 14,317 (NTSC) on its own frame. The tool has no phase for "one frame in N" (play, transition, init only). (Known before this build; the Cost line itself was corrected from 14,673 on the branch.)
2. **game-briefing, proposals from brief words.** Asked: `game-briefing` with a run-and-gun brief. Said (this brief): `raycaster_grid_walls` ("walls"), `tile_map_render` ("map"), `relocated_code_block` ("run", "block"), `multi_sprite_object`. True: none is needed; the map is raw screen codes, the soldier one sprite. The maintainer's earlier run of a different brief proposed `nav_area_pathfinding`, which this game does not need either (known before this build).
3. **plan-budget, the multiplexer counted twice.** Said: sprite_multiplex_game 8,995-16,600 and sprite_slot_parking 5,334, summed. True: parking is the slot policy of the one multiplexer (the parking page says so: "the slot policy for a game multiplexer whose slot count is fixed"); both figures hold a 16-slot sort. Measured: the whole logic frame, sort, build and every IRQ included, is 3,049 cycles at most (one sprite shown, 15 parked).
4. **check-compatibility, parking against the multiplexer.** Said: `unit_contention (hard)` sprite_multiplex_game x sprite_slot_parking on `sprite_0-7` and `vic_raster_irq`, verdict INCOMPATIBLE. True: they are one multiplexer here (mux.asm); the archetype's own fingerprint names both, so every plan from the archetype starts INCOMPATIBLE.
5. **check-compatibility, an implied tile renderer.** Said: `tile_map_render` implied as a prerequisite of char_attribute_flags (through tile_grid_collision). True: char_attribute_flags reads raw screen codes (its page, "How" step 2: "tile_map_render fills such an array", an example, not a need); this map has no tiles.
6. **pitfalls-for, the scroll techniques.** Asked: `pitfalls-for threshold_scroll_v` (and row_map_redraw, soft_scroll_v). Said: 16 pitfalls, among them `fpp_write_outside_window`, `linecrunch_write_outside_window`, `mid_row_badline_write_off_by_one`, `badline_every_line_block_length`, each "Reached through: SCROLY $D011 (Register), which this technique uses". True: none applies to a one-line-a-frame YSCROLL step; the register path pulls in every `$D011` trick.
7. **pitfalls-for, techniques it cannot find.** Asked: `pitfalls-for checkpoint_respawn`. Said: "No direct entity match", then BRK and NOP patch pitfalls by text score. `pitfalls-for grenade_lob`: "No direct match for grenade_lob". True: both are techniques in the graph (`technique-lookup` finds them); they have no pitfalls, which is a different answer from "no such thing".
8. **toolchain-hint, row_map_redraw.** Asked: `toolchain-hint row_map_redraw`. Said: Oscar64 by policy, and its "canonical snippet" is the page's Variations text, no code. True: the only recipe is KickAssembler; its figures depend on a patched `LDA abs,Y` / `STA abs,Y` loop in one page (14 cycles a byte, recipe row-map-redraw). The briefing's toolchain split also leaves row_map_redraw out of the KickAssembler parts. This starter's redraw is KickAssembler (kernel.asm `redraw`).
9. **Missing: how frame_sync_loop and row_map_redraw share a frame.** row_map_redraw says start the redraw after the last playfield badline (the band's tick, line 224) and write YSCROLL in the same frame; frame_sync_loop and the multiplexer want the loop to wake after the frame IRQ applies the frame's values (line 250 here). Neither page, nor the archetype ("on a frame that runs no other game logic"), says which frame is skipped or where the loop waits. Settled here by measurement (PLAN.md, "Decisions"): the logic frame commits, waits for the band's tick, redraws; the next frame starts late (line 135 PAL, 181 NTSC) and runs no logic.
10. **Missing: parking with the game multiplexer's reject.** sprite_slot_parking parks at Y 0, where each parked slot is fetched for 21 lines (105 cycles of DMA a frame on PAL, its page); the page does not say that with `sprite_multiplex_game`'s build, which stops at the first sorted Y past its cut, a parking Y below the cut costs no hardware sprite and no DMA. mux.asm parks at Y 255 below MAX_SY 187; the verdict's `SHOWN 1` (one sprite built with 15 slots parked) shows the reject drops them.
11. **Missing: the band IRQ entered late.** The invalid-mode-band and row-map-redraw listings poll with `CMP $D012 / BNE` for an exact line. Chained after a multiplexer's late guard, an entry after that line would spin a frame (pitfall `raster_poll_equality_misses_under_dispatch_latency`); neither recipe mentions it. kernel.asm polls "while below the line" (the same 7 cycles a pass, the same exit cycle) and `make phases` passes at all eight phases on both models.
12. **char_attribute_flags in multicolour.** Its page measures the draw-behind holes with a hires checker glyph. In multicolour text the `%01` pixels are background for priority too (techniques/sprite.md, mob_priority, measured there), so a canopy's `$D022` pixels show the sprite; the page does not say so. FIREBASE's canopy uses it: its light-green highlights show the soldier.
13. **The front end is not reached from the archetype.** Asked: `game-briefing ... --archetype vertical_run_and_gun` (PLAN.md, "Briefing") and `technique-lookup front_end_and_attract`, `technique-lookup game_state_machine`. Said: the briefing's 20 techniques hold no front-end, table or state-machine item; both lookups "not found" (they are game-design patterns, and no command looks a pattern up by name). Both pages' `**Applies to:**` lists ten archetypes and not `vertical_run_and_gun`. True: every shipped game of the archetype has a title, game over and table; `search "game state machine title play game over states"` finds both pages. Evidence: FIREBASE's front end (PLAN.md, "Front end") is built from them.
14. **Missing: joystick name entry.** Asked: `search "name entry with joystick letters"`; `front_end_and_attract` offers "text_input_line or a joystick letter wheel". Said: `text_input_line` only (GETIN, `$FFE4`); no technique or recipe for a letter wheel. True: a game that banks the KERNAL out (`$01 = $35`, as this one and the row-map-redraw recipe do) has no GETIN; its entry is the joystick. Built here: front.c `entry_frame` (edge detect, auto-repeat, fire/right accept, left back, a 1,000-frame timeout); `make fedrive` types DAB and B.. on the real `$DC00`.
15. **check-compatibility on the attract demo.** Asked: `check-compatibility high_score_table_insert decimal_print joystick_edge_detect joystick_autorepeat attract_mode_input_replay frame_sync_loop`. Said: `lfsr_random` implied as a prerequisite of attract_mode_input_replay, and `shared_register (soft)` "they write the same registers" for `DC00` between joystick_edge_detect, joystick_autorepeat and attract_mode_input_replay. True: the replay needs the seed only when the game draws random numbers (the page: "reproduces the run only if the game is deterministic from its seed"); FIREBASE has no generator and its demo repeats (`make fedrive`: DEMO after 400 frames, the title 485 later, every run). The three read `$DC00` and never write it (joystick_edge_detect's own Claims line says `cia1_port_a (reads)`).
16. **decimal_print's cost is its 16-bit route.** Asked: `technique-lookup decimal_print`. Said: 1,361 cycles a call (oscar64-print-number, the worst decimal case of a 16-bit value). True: the BCD variation it names is far cheaper and has no measured figure; a C `unsigned long % 10` print, which a starter's `put_dec` used, is far dearer. Measured here (make frontend, CIA1 timer B): a death frame's `flow_frame` (a 4-digit BCD add, six BCD digits and one byte digit printed) 836 cycles; 2,095 when the lives and grenade digits went through `put_dec` (`unsigned long`, `% 10`).
